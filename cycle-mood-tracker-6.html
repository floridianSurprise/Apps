ADHD & your cycle

Fluctuating oestrogen levels across the cycle can significantly affect how ADHD symptoms feel day to day — particularly focus, emotional regulation, and rejection sensitivity.

The clinical names for each stage are used elsewhere in the app in small print — here's what they actually mean, in order: your period, then the days just after, then mid-cycle, then the run-up to your next period.

Period
Menstrual · Days 1–5
Oestrogen and progesterone are at their lowest. Dopamine can drop, making brain fog, low motivation, and emotional sensitivity noticeably worse.
Just after
Follicular · Days 6–13
Rising oestrogen boosts dopamine and serotonin. Many people with ADHD report feeling sharper and more motivated. A good window for demanding tasks.
Mid-cycle
Ovulation · Days 14–16
Oestrogen peaks and testosterone rises briefly. Energy, confidence, and verbal fluency often feel strongest. Heightened sensory sensitivity is possible too.
Run-up to period
Luteal · Days 17–28
The final stretch before your period is when ADHD symptoms tend to spike hardest — rejection sensitivity, impulsivity, emotional dysregulation, executive dysfunction.

What this might mean for you

💊 MedicationSome people find their ADHD medication feels less effective in the run-up to their period. Worth tracking and raising with your prescriber if you notice a pattern.
📅 PlanningKnowing your "power window" — the days just after your period through to mid-cycle — can help you schedule demanding work and difficult conversations intentionally.
📊 EvidenceAfter a few months of logging, you'll have real data to show a GP, psychiatrist, or occupational health. Your tracker becomes evidence.
